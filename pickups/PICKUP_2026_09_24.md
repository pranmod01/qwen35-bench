# Pickup: 2026-09-24

## Session Summary (September 21–24)

Investigated the two anomalies flagged in `PICKUP_2026_09_21.md` (Open Items 1–2) and found both were
scoring artefacts, not model behaviour: the free-text parser mis-scored the paragraph replies the 2B/4B give
at mid study sizes, and `ThingsDataset` was not sampling randomly. Replaced text parsing with forced-choice
scoring (probability of "1" vs "2"), fixed the THINGS sampler, A/B-tested the prompt layout, and reran all
three models on all three tasks. **All 216 runs are complete and clean**; results are in
`results/qwen35_{0.8b,2b,4b}/summary.csv`. Nothing is committed yet (Open Item 4).

### Completed (this session)

1. **Root-caused Open Item 1 (PAM n=1 at 39–65%): the models named the object instead of recalling the word.**
   - Inspected `raw_response` in `results/archive_2026-09-21_pre_fix/qwen35_{2b,4b}/paired_associates/*n_images-1*`.
     Replies were clean single words — "HOCKEY", "MAGNIFYING GLASS", "NASCAR" — i.e. descriptions of the image,
     not the studied word. Not a parser problem.
   - Reworded the PAM test prompt in `bench/tasks/pam_eval.py` ("In the study list, which word was shown with
     this image? Do not name or describe the image.") and moved it after the image. n=1 went to 0.91–0.98.
   - Cost: the 0.8B got *worse* at Brady n=100 (0.63 → 0.37). Prompt sensitivity differs by model size; one
     prompt is used for all three for comparability (new Open Item 17).

2. **Root-caused Open Item 2 (4B recognition rising with study size): free-text parsing, plus a THINGS bug.**
   - At n=5–100 the 2B/4B ignore "reply with only the digit" and write paragraphs (up to 97% of trials in a
     cell). The parser then (a) scored a reply ending in "1" as unparseable because the reasoning contained
     "22 images", (b) scored replies *correct* off that same "22", and (c) lost answers truncated at
     `max_new_tokens=100`. Restricting the archived data to bare-digit replies showed the 4B at **1.00 at
     every size** — the dip was entirely scoring.
   - **`ThingsDataset(n_categories=k)` took the first k concept folders alphabetically**, so every THINGS n=1
     episode used *aardvark* and n=5 reused the same five concepts. Fixed in `src/stimuli.py` (shuffle with the
     eval seed). All THINGS results predating 2026-09-22 are void.

3. **Replaced text parsing with forced-choice scoring, verified against a control.**
   - `Qwen35Evaluator.score_choices()` (`bench/models.py`) prefills `Answer: ` and reads p("1") vs p("2") at
     that position. Each trial stores `choice_probs`, `choice_mass`, `top_tokens`, plus the free-text reply
     (`free_response`, `free_parsed`) which no longer affects any score.
   - Two bugs found and fixed along the way: summing both tokenisations put the shared leading-space token
     (p=0.997) in every choice's set, making every trial read exactly 0.5000 (`diag_choice.py`, job 12803143);
     and `model(**inputs)` materialised logits for every position (~30 GiB at 250 THINGS images), OOMing 16
     runs until `logits_to_keep=1` was added.
   - Control (`$ZROOT/tmp/control_2afc.py`, job 12803703, 20 trials/cell): with a study sequence containing
     neither test image, 2B/4B score 0.45–0.65 and answer "1" on 18–20 of 20; on real trials 0.85–1.00 with
     balanced answers. The scorer reads memory, not position or image statistics.

4. **A/B-tested the prompt layout; `original` won and is now pinned in all configs.**
   - `configs/prompt_ab.yaml`, jobs 12819745-50, 3 models x 2 tasks x 2 layouts, 50 episodes/cell.
   - The `labeled` layout ("Image 1:"/"Image 2:" + question last), introduced to stop the paragraphs, pairs
     "first" with "1" and biases answers: 4B serial order n=2 **1.00 → 0.62**, 2B **0.90 → 0.44**. Recognition
     was within noise for the 4B, mixed for the smaller models.
   - Prompt is now a config key (`prompts: [original|labeled]`), part of each condition and result filename;
     a config without it raises. All three model configs pin `original`. Since forced choice ignores the text,
     verbosity is no longer a reason to touch the prompt.

   - **2B recognition** peaks near n=100 (0.83–0.84) and decays to ~0.55 at 500: real degradation, with a

6. **Automatic quality flags, with thresholds fixed before the rerun** (`bench/summarize.py`).
   - `LOW_MASS`: >10% of a cell's trials put <0.9 probability on "1"+"2". Cause identified from `top_tokens`:
     the models answer with a **serial position** instead ("5": 0.32 beating both digits), which is why it
     clusters at n=5. 9 cells flagged. It questions the measurement, not necessarily the number — the 4B's
     flagged serial-order n=5 cells still score 0.88–0.96.
   - `BIAS`: one answer on >80% of trials. Mostly where accuracy is already chance; the misleading exceptions
     are 0.8B recognition Brady n=1 (0.71 = says "2" on 83% of trials) and serial order n=2 (0.71/0.63).
   - Accuracy stays raw — nothing dropped or reweighted. `--simple` prints accuracy only.

7. **Tested and rejected the resolution hypothesis for the 0.8B** (`configs/img_resolution.yaml`, job 12867560).
   - The 0.8B scores 0.90 on THINGS n=1 but is at chance on Brady. Shrinking THINGS to 256 px (Brady's native
     size, ~68 tokens/image) changed nothing: **0.94 at 256 px vs 0.92 at 512 px**, answers balanced at both.
     Brady, the control, scored identically under both settings.
   - So neither pixel count nor visual token count explains it. Remaining hypothesis: stimulus distinctiveness
     (Brady = isolated objects on white, shared layout; THINGS = natural photos differing on many dimensions).
     Untested (Open Item 14).
   - Retracted an earlier claim from this session: the 0.8B does *not* have a novelty preference. Its
     below-chance Brady scores (0.14 at n=25) came from the `labeled` prompt; under `original` it is at chance.
