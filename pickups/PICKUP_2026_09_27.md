2. **The 9B's low serial-order accuracy is an asymmetric response bias, not worse memory** (the "chase"
   asked for this session).
   - Split by which side was correct (Brady2008; hit1 = accuracy when the answer was "1"):

     | Model | n=2 | 5 | 25 | 100 | 250 | 500 |
     |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
     | 4B hit1 / hit2 | 1.00 / **1.00** | 0.83 / 0.92 | 0.98 / 0.56 | 0.98 / 0.08 | 1.00 / 0.06 | 1.00 / 0.02 |
     | 9B hit1 / hit2 | 1.00 / **0.48** | 1.00 / 0.28 | 1.00 / 0.37 | 1.00 / 0.10 | 1.00 / 0.11 | 0.98 / 0.04 |

   - Both models hold hit1 near 1.00 while hit2 collapses: they assert the first image shown in the test
     pair is the earlier one. The 4B has a balanced regime at n=2–5 and degrades into the bias by n=100;
     **the 9B is already in it at n=2**. Free-text agreement with the forced choice is 83–100%, so it is
     the model's answer, not a scoring artefact.
   - That `first`→`1` pairing is exactly what the `labeled` layout produced for the 2B/4B in the
     2026-09-22 prompt A/B, which is why `original` was pinned — and the 9B shows it under `original`.
     Prompt sensitivity differs by model size in both directions.
   - **Consequence for reporting: serial order needs hit1/hit2, not accuracy.** Accuracy ≈ 0.50 there
     means "always says 1" for every model, which is not the same as chance-level memory.
   - **The prompt A/B settled it: the prior is the model's own, not the layout's** (job 12946926,
     `configs/serial_order_ab_9b.yaml`, 50 episodes per cell). Brady2008 hit1/hit2:

     | Layout | n=2 | 5 | 25 | 100 |
     |---|---|---|---|---|
     | `original` | 1.00 / 0.50 | 1.00 / 0.29 | 1.00 / 0.33 | 1.00 / 0.19 |
     | `labeled` | 1.00 / 0.21 | 1.00 / 0.57 | 0.91 / 0.44 | 0.91 / 0.04 |

     `labeled` helps at n=5 and hurts at n=2 and n=100; on THINGS it is equal or worse everywhere (at
     n=100 it says "1" on 100% of trials). The pre-registered rule required hit2 to rise at small n
     without hit1 collapsing, so `original` stays pinned and the prior is reported as a model property.
     The A/B also replicates the main sweep on fresh episodes (0.72/0.60/0.64 vs 0.74/0.62/0.64).
